# CHAPTER 1 — "WHAT IS PUBERTY?" (v2 COLD OPEN)

**Setting:** Mia's bedroom, 4:47 PM on a Tuesday. Evidence board. Fairy lights. One very serious ten-year-old.

---

Okay. Quick briefing, because things are getting SERIOUSLY weird in my house.

**Item 1:** My sister Solani — age twelve, owner of approximately nine hundred butterfly clips — has started saying "I need SPACE" like she's an astronaut. Yesterday she said it to the BATHROOM DOOR.

**Item 2:** She has a secret snack stash. I found it. Where? In a shoebox marked "DO NOT TOUCH, MIA," which, honestly, is basically an engraved invitation.

**Item 3:** There's a song she only listens to with headphones on, and when Mom asks what it is, she says "NOTHING" in a voice that sounds like a slammed door wearing a hoodie.

**Item 4:** Mom and Tío Mo keep doing **The Look**. You know the one. The "our little girl is becoming a—" and then they stop talking because I walk in. RUDE. I'm ten, not invisible.

So I've started an official investigation:

# OPERATION SISTERWATCH

Because whatever's happening to Solani? It's heading down the hall toward ME next. And I like to be PREPARED. Like a scout. A scout with an evidence board and a worry notebook and three best friends who are extremely bad at keeping secrets but extremely good at snacks.

Oh — hi. You're here.

Yes, **YOU.** I see you reading this during silent reading time, thinking you're slick. Don't worry. This book is way cooler than whatever book-shaped nap Mr. Whoever handed you. Stick with me. I'll explain everything.

*[text thread: THE MYTH MACHINE 🕵🏾♀️]*

**MIA:** emergency meeting. my room. bring snacks. solani did something new.

**JADA:** 👀 ON MY WAY. bring on the drama!!

**EMMA:** do I need my sketchbook?

**MIA:** obviously. we need a DIAGRAM.

---

Twenty minutes later, the Myth Machine was in session.

"It's definitely aliens," Jada announced, upside down on my bed. "I saw a movie exactly like this. The big sister starts acting weird, next thing you know—"

"Jada, your last three theories were aliens."

"Alien ADJACENT."

Emma chewed her pencil. "My cousin says when girls get to a certain age they get… you know. The *thing*."

"What thing?" I demanded.

"The thing where they need deodorant," she whispered. "And not the fun kind. The SERIOUS kind. She said it's like armpit perfume, but for fancy people."

I wrote it on the board in huge letters:

## MYTH #1: DEODORANT IS ARMPIT PERFUME FOR FANCY PEOPLE

"Then my sister's hiding FANCY," I said, "and we have to find out why."

The door burst open. It was Mema Hazel, halfway through a show tune, carrying a plate of something that smelled like heaven and cinnamon.

"FANCY?" Mema said, mid-spin. "Baby, the only thing fancy in this house is my Sunday hat." She set down the plate, adjusted her cat-eye glasses (which were, as always, on top of her head), and pointed at the board. "Y'all need to know this: bodies sweat. Everybody's. It's called being alive. And deodorant ain't perfume — it's just backup. Soap is the star. Deodorant's just the understudy."

I blinked. "So… it's not fancy?"

"Girl, it's from the SAME drugstore as the gum."

Jada gasped like I'd never heard her gasp. "The gum?! No way."

Mema winked at me, already bopping toward the door. "You keep investigating, sugar. But when you investigate SOLANI, remember — every girl walks her own hallway. Even nosy little sisters."

She left, humming.

I stared at my board. Somewhere down the hall, Solani's door opened a crack, then shut.

**Something was definitely going on. And I was going to find out what.**
