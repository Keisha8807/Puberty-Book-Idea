## 1. SEASON THREE-BULLET PROMISE

### Who Season 1 helps

Black girls approximately ages 9–11 approaching or beginning puberty—especially readers who are curious, embarrassed, worried that they are early/late, or reluctant to ask a grown-up.

### How Season 1 helps

Mia turns Solani's changes into a funny investigation. The reader learns through Operation Sisterwatch, the Myth Machine, sister conflict, trusted Black adults, practical preparation, and story-based myth correction.

### Season 1 transformation

The reader moves from:

> “Something weird and scary is happening, and growing up might erase who I am.”

To:

> “Puberty happens on different schedules. I can understand it, prepare for it, ask for help, and still be fully myself.”

### Parent/caregiver trust promise

> A funny, culturally affirming story that helps a Black girl begin puberty conversations with practical language, trusted-adult pathways, and guidance that will be sourced and medically reviewed before publication—without shame, fear tactics, or treating one timeline as universal.

This is the fixed buyer/trust target for later caregiver feedback. Reviewers should test whether the outline earns this promise rather than inventing a different promise for the book.

## 2. SEASON ARC

### Opening state

Mia believes knowledge equals control. If she can solve Solani, she can prevent puberty from surprising her.

### Midseason turn

Mia learns that preparation is not permission to invade someone else's privacy. Facts matter, but so do boundaries, feelings, and the dignity of the person experiencing the change.

### Closing state

Mia's preparation is tested when her own first period begins. She uses the support system built across the season and discovers that a body milestone does not instantly make her older, different, or less silly.

### Approved finale lock — AQ-009A

Keisha approved the governing Season 1 spine and order: **Mia begins by investigating Solani, progresses through the 11 cards shown here, and gets her own first period in Episode 11.** This is a character-specific event and payoff—not a promise that every 10-year-old reader will begin at the same age.

# 4. LEVEL 1 EPISODE FLOW CARDS

## EPISODE 1 — THE MYSTERY NEXT DOOR

### Concept

Mia mistakes the signs of puberty for evidence of a covert takeover.

### Premise

At 4:47 on a Tuesday, Mia presents an evidence board proving that Solani has become suspiciously different. She recruits the reader and calls the first emergency Myth Machine meeting.

### Short synopsis

The approved cold open introduces Solani's demand for space, snack stash, secret song, and the adults' mysterious Looks. Mia, Jada, and Emma escalate from aliens to disease to “fancy deodorant.” Mema busts the deodorant myth, then recognizes that Mia's investigation is driven by fear that she is next. Mema gives the case its real name—puberty—and explains that it is gradual growth, not an instant takeover. Mia opens an official puberty case file while a fragment of Solani's secret song passes through the closed bedroom door.

### Mia's want

Identify what happened to Solani and get ahead of it.

### Hidden fear

Growing up may take over her personality and remove her childhood before she agrees.

### Educational promise

The reader understands a broad, age-appropriate definition of puberty and knows it happens on individual schedules.

### Reader transformation

“Puberty is an alien/disease mystery” → “Puberty has a name, takes time, and can be understood.”

### Myth Machine theories

- Puberty is alien adjacent.
- Puberty is a contagious disease.
- Puberty is optional if you refuse the update.
- Deodorant is armpit perfume for fancy people.

### Trusted truth-giver

Mema Hazel—warm enough to see the fear and funny enough to keep Mia listening.

### Ask Mema question

“What is puberty—and does it happen all at once?”

### Five plot beats

1. Mia presents four suspicious changes in Solani.
2. The reader becomes her case partner; Jada and Emma arrive.
3. The Myth Machine produces increasingly wrong explanations.
4. Mema busts the first myth and names Mia's real fear.
5. Puberty receives a simple definition; Mia opens the official case.

### Season thread

First muffled fragment of Solani's private song. No CROWN explanation.

### Stinger

The bathroom-door mystery, the chocolate emergency, and why Mia's chest hurts when she runs.

## EPISODE 2 — BODY UNDER CONSTRUCTION

### Concept

Mia discovers that “normal” growth does not mean symmetrical, overnight, or painless.

### Premise

During gym, running suddenly makes Mia's chest feel tender. She treats the sensation like structural damage and tries to hide it while collecting evidence.

### Short synopsis

Mia's attempt to avoid running draws Coach Armstrong's attention. Jada suggests a growth spurt is supposed to happen all at once; Emma diagrams a perfectly symmetrical body timeline. Nurse Nightingale explains that developing bodies do not follow identical calendars and that breast budding/tenderness can be a normal change while pain and concerns should still be shared with a trusted adult. Brenda follows up at home without rushing Mia into the later bra-shopping story. Solani quietly gives Mia a practical comfort tip, revealing care beneath the roast.

### Mia's want

Prove she is not changing—or at least that nothing is wrong.

### Hidden fear

She is early, broken, or becoming visibly different before her friends.

### Educational promise

Growth varies in order, pace, and symmetry; tenderness and questions are valid reasons to seek adult/clinical support.

### Reader transformation

“My body must match a perfect schedule” → “My body can change in its own order, and I can ask questions.”

### Myth Machine theories

- Growth happens overnight.
- Both sides always develop at exactly the same time.
- Tenderness automatically means something is seriously wrong.
- Talking about a change makes it happen faster.

### Trusted truth-givers

Coach Armstrong notices without shaming; Nurse Nightingale explains; Brenda provides follow-through.

### Ask Mema / trusted-adult question

“How do I know whether a body change is typical or something I should tell an adult?”

### Five plot beats

1. Tenderness interrupts gym.
2. Mia hides and invents a structural emergency.
3. Jada/Emma create the “perfect growth timeline.”
4. Nurse/Coach normalize variation and adult help.
5. Brenda and Solani make support feel practical, not dramatic.

### Season thread

Solani helps without becoming the teacher of the whole book.

### Stinger

Solani cries at a juice commercial and denies everything.

## EPISODE 3 — THE FEELINGS FORECAST

### Concept

Mia treats emotions like faulty weather equipment until she recognizes her own storm.

### Premise

Solani cries at a harmless commercial, then laughs minutes later. Mia declares an emotional malfunction.

### Short synopsis

The Myth Machine builds an absurd feelings forecast, assigning every emotion a fake emergency level. At school, Mia overreacts to a small disappointment and tries to pretend it did not count. Ms. Harmony helps the girls distinguish feelings from actions: emotions carry information, but they do not justify hurting others. Mia recognizes that Solani's reactions are not evidence of a broken personality—and that her own feelings also deserve language. A glimpse of Solani's lyric notebook suggests that humor is not her only emotional outlet.

### Mia's want

Repair Solani's “mood glitch.”

### Hidden fear

Big feelings mean losing control or becoming impossible to love.

### Educational promise

Emotional intensity can change during puberty; feelings are valid, actions still carry responsibility, and coping/support skills matter.

### Reader transformation

“Big feelings mean broken” → “Feelings are information I can name, express, and manage.”

### Myth Machine theories

- Mood changes mean a person is broken.
- Crying automatically means sadness.
- Strong feelings are attention-seeking.
- If a feeling is real, every thought attached to it must be true.

### Trusted truth-giver

Ms. Harmony, with Mema as a brief home callback rather than a second lecture.

### Ask Mema question

“Why can feelings change so fast—and what can I do before I react?”

### Five plot beats

1. Solani's juice-commercial tears trigger the case.
2. Myth Machine creates a feelings forecast.
3. Mia experiences her own emotional spike.
4. Ms. Harmony separates feeling, thought, and action.
5. Mia gives Solani space and names her own emotion honestly.

### Season thread

Lyric notebook glimpse only; Mia does not read it.

### Stinger

Picture day turns Mia's mirror into an unreliable witness.

## EPISODE 4 — MIRROR, MIRROR, MAYBE LIAR

### Concept

Mia searches for the one “true” reflection and learns that images never carry her whole worth.

### Premise

On school-picture morning, every mirror, phone camera, and reflection seems to show a different Mia.

### Short synopsis

Mia tries lighting, angles, expressions, and increasingly ridiculous mirror tests. Mia reads Kayla's polished confidence as a standard she must match, intensifying the comparison without making Kayla an objective measure of beauty. Marcus's camera demonstrates that one image captures one moment, while Brenda helps Mia name the pressure beneath her jokes. Mia chooses a picture expression that feels like herself rather than the one she thinks will be judged best. The episode protects body positivity without forcing instant self-love.

### Mia's want

Find the objectively prettiest and most “correct” version of herself.

### Hidden fear

Body changes will make her less recognizable, less pretty, or more available for public judgment.

### Educational promise

Appearance changes and comparison can affect body image; photos, mirrors, trends, and comments are limited perspectives—not measures of worth.

### Reader transformation

“There is one right way to look” → “Images are partial; my body and worth are not public votes.”

### Myth Machine theories

- Mirrors always tell the complete truth.
- Pretty has one formula.
- A bad photo proves a bad appearance.
- Comparison motivates everyone in a healthy way.

### Trusted truth-giver

Brenda for emotional truth; Marcus for the image/camera metaphor.

### Ask Mema question

“What do I do when I don't like what I see today?”

### Five plot beats

1. Picture-day mirror panic.
2. Mia runs tests and compares herself with Kayla.
3. Myth Machine attempts a beauty formula.
4. Marcus/Brenda expose the limits of images and comparison.
5. Mia chooses an honest expression rather than a perfect mask.

### Season thread

Marcus's camera becomes a family/world prop, not an authority on beauty.

### Stinger

Solani catches Mia touching something marked private.

## EPISODE 5 — OPERATION PERSONAL SPACE

### Concept

Mia learns that solving a mystery does not give her ownership of another person's private life.

At school, Kayla controls a group project until Sophie tries to speak, giving Mia a second view of what ignored boundaries feel like. ### Mia's want

Keep unrestricted access to Solani and every clue.

### Hidden fear

A boundary means rejection or reduced love.

### Educational promise

Boundaries support safety and relationships; girls can set, respect, and revise boundaries around bodies, belongings, space, and conversations.

### Reader transformation

“Boundaries are rude walls” → “Boundaries communicate respect and help relationships survive change.”

### Myth Machine theories

- Family members cannot need privacy.
- Boundaries end friendships.
- Only adults can set boundaries.
- A past yes means permanent permission.

### Trusted truth-givers

Solani provides lived impact; Ms. Harmony provides language and practice.

### Ask Mema question

“How can I ask for space without making someone think I don't love them?”

### Five plot beats

1. Mia crosses the bedroom-door boundary.
2. Solani sets a consequence and withdraws.
3. School group conflict repeats the power problem.
4. Ms. Harmony teaches clear boundary language.
5. Mia makes a no-excuse apology and tolerates waiting.

### Season thread

### Adult-load and reader-attachment decision

The season intentionally gives Mia a **support web**, not one all-purpose puberty guru. The primary recurring attachment anchors are Brenda (caregiver), Nurse Nightingale (school health), and Solani (sister impact). ## Episode-shape and rhythm decision

A recognizable case rhythm is intentional for the 9–11 reader and the “animated show on paper” format. The recurring engine is:

> Disruptive clue → funny theory/attempt → consequence or complication → trustworthy context → Mia's child-led decision/new normal

It is a **rhythm, not a mandatory adult-correction formula**. Mia must act; adults may provide context or support but cannot conveniently solve her central emotional problem.

Two emotional high points deliberately bend the pattern:

- **Episode 8:** Mia remembers the Episode 5 boundary lesson and interrupts her own public reveal **before** Brenda explains menstruation. Her ethical choice creates the safe conditions for truth; the adult does not stop a completed humiliation.
- **Episode 11:** The episode opens at the season's bodily payoff, skips a fresh comic theory spiral, and lets Mia use accumulated knowledge to choose her next safe action before Nurse Nightingale, Brenda, and Solani support her. The ending resolves identity fear rather than simply closing a case.

Detailed packets should also vary who initiates the disruption, where the truth enters, whether a theory is spoken or acted out, and whether the final beat is comic, tender, or unresolved. Do not force every episode into **mystery → wrong theories → adult lecture → feeling → tidy resolution**.
