The songs I would actually develop first

I wouldn't write 15 full songs. That risks making the campaign feel like a musical when the actual franchise identity is case-file comedy + trusted puberty guidance. The trailer plan says the central promise is that something strange has a name, can be understood/prepared for, and doesn't erase who the girl is.

I'd build a small Mia musical universe around six particularly strong ideas:

“Nobody Gave Me the Manual” — #3
The funny, highly relatable kid-facing single. Upbeat pop/R&B with Mia's investigative personality.
“Myth Machine” — #5
The franchise earworm. Short, repeatable and adaptable: every new myth can become another verse/reel.
“Feelings Are Weather” — #7
The educational song that doesn't sound like educational music. This has classroom/SEL possibilities beyond the trailer.
“The Whole Day” — #8
The body-image song. The line “A photo is one second / you are the whole day” is unusually song-friendly because it gives you both an image and a hook.
“Still Me” — #11
The Season 1 anthem. This deserves the most songwriting attention because it embodies the actual season transformation: a body milestone doesn't suddenly turn Mia into someone else.
“A Way In” — #15
The parent/daughter campaign song. Two voices or two lyrical perspectives could converge in the chorus, mirroring “She gets a story / You get a starting sentence.”

And I'd make #2 “Growing Into Me” a seventh only if you want an overall Mia's Puberty Primer theme song that can live beyond Season 1.

There is also an important distinction between a song for girls 9–11 and a song that sounds like children's music. I would aim for the former. Contemporary clean pop, R&B/pop, light funk, handclaps, playful bass, bright synths and occasional cinematic/spy textures can feel aspirational to a 9–11-year-old without sounding preschool-ish. Mia should sound like she's entering the next stage of childhood—not like the music is trying to make her older than she is.

The uploaded songwriting guide is useful here conceptually too: its opening frames lyric structure around controlling things such as phrase number, phrase length, rhythm and rhyme arrangement, rather than treating structure as decoration. For these trailers especially, I'd exploit that by writing the strongest songs hook-first: the chorus/title should contain the trailer's central transformation in language a girl can remember after one listen.

One more thing I'd protect strongly: “Still Me” should not be Solani's secret song. Your flow grid specifically says Solani's music is atmospheric rather than plot-active and locks it to introduce → deepen → payoff, with no full song. Marketing music can be its own soundtrack universe, which lets us make great songs without breaking that mystery.

If we develop these, I’d start with “Nobody Gave Me the Manual,” “Myth Machine,” and “Still Me.” Those three give you the comedy, franchise identity, and emotional heart of the series respectively.

what song should be the theme song

I’d make “Growing Into Me” the official theme song for the entire Mia’s Puberty Primer series.

It has the right scope. Your series promise isn't ultimately about periods, mirrors, feelings, or any single puberty topic; it's about a girl learning that her body can change on its own schedule while she remains fully herself. That is exactly what “Growing Into Me” communicates. The Season 1 flow grid defines the transformation as moving from fear that growing up might erase who Mia is to understanding that she can learn, prepare, ask for help, and “still be fully myself.”

More importantly, its hook works as brand language:

I'm growing into me
Little by little, clue by clue...
My own timing isn't wrong.

“Clue by clue” connects directly to Mia's detective/case-file identity without locking the song specifically to Operation Sisterwatch. That means the song can survive future seasons.

I’d give the songs different jobs rather than asking one song to do everything:

Song	Role
⭐ Growing Into Me	Official series theme / musical identity
Something's Going On	Operation Sisterwatch / mystery motif
Nobody Gave Me the Manual	Main kid-facing promotional single
Myth Machine	Recurring franchise segment song
Feelings Are Weather	Episode/topic song
The Whole Day	Body-image episode/topic song
Pack a Little Plan	Period-preparation episode/topic song
Still Me	Season 1 finale anthem
A Way In	Parent/daughter campaign song
Dear Ten-Year-Old Me	Caregiver/emotional promotional song
Ask Mema!	Recurring segment sonic logo

The distinction between “Growing Into Me” and “Still Me” is especially important.

Growing Into Me = the journey.
Still Me = the Season 1 payoff.

If you make “Still Me” the overall theme, you're starting with the conclusion. It becomes much more powerful after we've watched Mia spend the season worrying that puberty might change who she is. The finale then earns that song.

I would actually build the opening/closing branding around “Growing Into Me.” The full version can exist as the soundtrack single, while trailers and animated episodes use a recognizable 5–10 second instrumental signature derived from its melody. Then, for a major series trailer, you can hit the recognizable chorus:

I'm growing into me...

That phrase also has excellent potential beyond music: trailer end card, series tagline, website hero, educator materials, social campaign hashtag/theme, and eventually even live/read-along programming.

So if we are choosing one song that should become synonymous with Mia's Puberty Primer, my vote is firmly:

🎵 GROWING INTO ME

Official Theme of Mia’s Puberty Primer

And I would preserve “Still Me” as the emotional weapon you don't fully deploy until the Season 1 ending.

what 7-11 second clips of the lyrics should be part of the trailers if im asking correctly

Yes—you’re asking exactly the right question. The full songs are soundtrack assets; the trailers need a 7–11 second “money moment” from each song. I’d usually choose the lyric that delivers the trailer’s emotional idea immediately, rather than automatically using the beginning of the chorus.

For your 15 trailers, I’d cut them like this:

#	Trailer	7–11 sec lyric/audio moment	How I’d use it
1	Evidence Board	“Something’s going on / Something’s going on / I don’t know what yet / But I’m finding out”	⭐ Excellent. Whisper starts over clues; beat lands on “finding out.”
2	Pixar Teaser	“I’m growing into me / Curious, silly, completely me / I can change and still belong / My own timing isn’t wrong”	⭐ This is the theme-song reveal. Logo lands on “Growing Into Me.”
3	Relatable Crisis POV	“Nobody gave me the manual / Nobody gave me the map / Growing is kind of unusual / What’s up with this? What’s up with that?”	⭐ Probably your strongest kid-facing trailer clip. Immediate recognition + humor.
4	Before the Internet	Instrumental first, then only: “Questions need somewhere to go.”	Don't fill all 10 seconds with singing. Parent-facing copy/VO needs breathing room.
5	Myth Machine	“Got a theory? / Run it through the Myth Machine! / Sounds suspicious? / Run it through the Myth Machine!”	⭐ Fantastic recurring social hook. I'd prioritize this over the longer chorus.
6	Sister Reveal	“A closed door / Isn't goodbye.” + instrumental	Keep it mysterious. Don't explain the relationship through lyrics.
7	Feelings Forecast	“Feelings are weather / They roll in, they roll out / Sunny, stormy, somewhere in between”	⭐ Nearly perfect trailer lyric. Animate the weather to the words.
8	Mirror	“A picture is one second / I'm the whole day / More than one reflection / More than what one camera says”	⭐ One of the campaign's best 10-second excerpts. Camera flash on “one second.”
9	Boundary	“That is not yours.” → instrumental pause → “I can ask / I can listen / I can wait.”	⭐ Let the silence do some work. This shouldn't feel like a music video.
10	Period Kit	“Pack a little plan / Not the whole bedroom / Just what helps me feel ready / And a little extra room”	⭐ Perfect for objects dropping into the pouch on the beat.
11	Still Mia	“Something new can happen / And I can still be the same / I'm learning, I'm growing / I'm becoming at my speed”	Strong, but I'd save the actual “I'm still me” payoff for the final frame.

A major point: don't make every trailer 10 seconds of uninterrupted singing. Your trailer plan says to win the first three seconds, use short phrases, and keep the book visible before the end. The music should support that architecture rather than consume it.

I'd actually divide the 15 into three editing treatments.

Full lyric hook: #2, #3, #5, #7, #8, #10, #11, #14, #15. These have lyrics strong enough to be the trailer copy.

Tiny vocal motif + score: #1 and #6. These establish atmosphere and franchise identity without turning the trailer into a music video.

Primarily instrumental: #4, #9 and #13. Their spoken/text moments are more important than lyrics. #12 is its own category because it's a sonic logo.

And there are five clips I would treat as your premium “money hooks”:

“Nobody gave me the manual…”
Girl recognition.

“Feelings are weather…”
Educational recognition.

“A picture is one second / I'm the whole day…”
Emotional recognition.

“I'm growing into me…”
Franchise recognition.

“I get a story / I get a start…”
Parent + daughter recognition.

That also prevents the songs from feeling like random soundtrack choices. Each excerpt is performing a specific marketing job.

One additional production decision I'd make: create dedicated 7–11 second trailer mixes instead of simply chopping the mastered songs. A trailer mix can start directly on the hook, bring the bass/drums in immediately, shorten pauses, add a final logo hit, and resolve musically at exactly 8, 9, or 10 seconds. That will feel dramatically more intentional than fading out halfway through a chorus.
