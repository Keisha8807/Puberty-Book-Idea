## 3. PAGE-LEVEL DESIGN LANGUAGE (how "not stiff" becomes instructions)

Your 74-page Canva draft already uses a **hand-drawn friendly style** (the original filename even says "Hand Drawn Style"!). Build on it with these rules:

1. **Every chapter = an episode.** It starts with a COLD OPEN — mid-action, mid-sentence, something already happening ("So there I was, armpit deep in drama..."). Not "In this chapter we will learn..."

2. **Mia breaks the fourth wall.** Asides to the reader, side-eyes to the camera, "yes, YOU. I see you reading this." — Proud Family + Georgia both do this, and it kills stiffness instantly.

3. **The margins are alive.** Doodles, arrows, crossed-out words, "Mema says..." sticky notes, Jada's commentary scribbled in. Wimpy Kid energy.

4. **Fact Check = a cutaway gag, not a sidebar.** In the show, the scene freezes and a little "🔍 FACT CHECK" card pops in — maybe Mia's glasses flash, maybe the health teacher walks in for 10 seconds. On the page: a bold card with a character reaction, not a gray box.

5. **Mema Hazel Explains = a scene, not a lecture.** Zone 2 gets styled like a cutaway to grandma's kitchen: Mema mid-routine (rollers in her hair, talking to her plants), breaking down the facts HER way. Education delivered through character.

6. **End every chapter with a "next time on..." stinger.** Your cliffhanger idea, but framed like a TV tease: *"Next time on Mia's Puberty Adventure: a locker room emergency and a bra with TWELVE hooks?!"* → this is also how the missing Bra/Crush/Social chapters get teased into a Book 2 if you want them.

7. **The book doubles as a storyboard.** You already own: Mia's 11-expression character sheet (from the Consistent Character GPT), the Laughter Currency animation script, and a hand-drawn Canva system. Lay pages out like keyframes — because the animated series isn't a *different* project, it's the same pages, moving.
