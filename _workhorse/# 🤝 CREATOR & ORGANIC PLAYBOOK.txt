## 0. Why this beats the ad budget
You have $280 for ads. One good micro-creator share can outperform all of it.
| | Paid ads at your budget | One right micro-creator |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | ~10–20k impressions total | 8–40k, already gathered |
| Trust | Zero — you're a stranger interrupting | Borrowed — she vouched for you |
| Cost | $1–3 per email | $0 (gifted) or 40% of a sale that wouldn't exist |
| Half-life | Dies when spend stops | Post stays up; her comments keep working |
| Bonus | — | Content you can repost, a testimonial, sometimes a friend-of-a-friend chain |
**The catch:** it's slow, it's rejection-heavy, and *you* have to send the messages. Budget 30 minutes a week and expect most people to ignore you. The ones who don't are worth it.

## 1. Micro, not macro — and why
| Tier | Followers | Typical engagement | Reality for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nano** | 1k–10k | 4–8% | ⭐ **Best fit.** Answers her own DMs. Will genuinely read your kit. Often a mom exactly like your buyer. |
| **Micro** | 10k–50k | 2–5% | ⭐ **Target zone.** Big enough to move 20–60 emails, small enough to reply to you. |
| **Mid** | 50k–250k | 1–3% | Occasionally reachable. Usually wants payment. Try 3–5, don't count on them. |
| **Macro** | 250k+ | <1% | Ignore. Has a manager, a rate card, and no reason to care yet. |
Engagement *inverts* with size. A 6k-follower mom whose comments are full of other moms of 10-year-olds is worth more to you than a 200k lifestyle account.

## 3. Building the list of 60
**Tool routing:** Claude in Chrome researches (read-only, logged in) → Cowork writes each profile into the tracker → **you** decide who to contact.
Search patterns that work:
- Hashtag → sort by recent, not top (top is macro)
- **The comment-section trick:** open a post from a creator you like, read *her commenters* — many are nano-creators in the same niche and far more reachable
- Pinterest: search "puberty talk daughter," "period kit for tween" → click through to profiles
- TPT: search "puberty," "growing up," "girls SEL" → look at seller storefronts → most link an Instagram
- YouTube: "talking to my daughter about puberty" → check channel sizes under 50k
**Disqualify immediately:** anyone posting their child's body, anyone giving medical advice without credentials, anyone whose comments are hostile, anyone with obviously bought followers (huge count, 4 comments).
### Tracker schema — `_workhorse/02-inbox/marketing/creator-tracker.csv`
```
name, handle, platform, followers, archetype, why_them, personal_detail,
contact_method, date_contacted, response, offer_made, posted_date,
emails_driven, sales_driven, notes
```
`personal_detail` is the most important column. It's the sentence in your DM that proves you're not a bot.

## 6. Outreach templates
Personalize the first line of every single one. If you can't, don't send it.
### Template A — cold DM (nano/micro, Instagram)
> Hi [Name] — your post about [SPECIFIC THING, e.g. "your daughter asking why her body was changing before her friends'"] stopped me. That's exactly the conversation I froze on with mine.
>
> I'm a mom in Albany writing a story-first book for Black girls about growing up — the kind I wish I'd had instead of the clinical pamphlet.
>
> I made a free one-page "how to start the talk" kit for parents. Can I send it to you? No ask attached — if it's useful to your people you're welcome to share it, and if not, you've got a free kit. 💛
**Why it works:** specific, gives before asking, one clear yes/no, no link in the first message (links tank DM deliverability).
### Template B — educator / nurse / librarian (email)
> Subject: A free puberty-talk resource for your [families / students / patrons]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Keisha, a mom in Albany writing *[title]* — a story-first middle-grade book for Black girls about puberty, built around a character named Mia and her family. Not a clinical handbook; a story with the facts folded in.
>
> I saw [SPECIFIC THING you do]. I've put together a free caregiver conversation kit and I'd love your eyes on it — both to share with your [families/students] if it's useful, and honestly because feedback from someone who does this work daily would make the book better.
>
> No cost, no catch. Would you like me to send it over?
>
> — Keisha [link to landing page]
⭐ **For nurses/health educators, add:** *"I'm also looking for a health professional to review the book's factual content as a paid consultant — if that's of interest, I'd love to talk."* This is how you solve two problems with one email.
### Template C — the follow-up (send once, 7 days later, then stop)
> Hi [Name] — bumping this once in case it got buried, then I'll leave you alone. 😊 Still happy to send the kit over if it's useful.
### Template D — the ask, after she's said yes to the kit
> So glad it landed! One thing if you're open to it: I'm launching a Founding Reader bundle on Black Friday. If you'd ever want to share it, I'd set you up with 40% commission and your own code for your people. Totally fine either way — the kit's yours regardless.

## 7. Cadence and realistic math
**10 outreach messages per week, starting Block 2 (Sep 22).** That's ~30 minutes on Friday. Ten weeks = ~100 contacts, comfortably past your 60.
Expect this funnel:
```
60 contacted
  → 20 reply                    (33% — good for cold, personalized)
  → 10 accept the kit
  →  4 actually post            (this is normal, not failure)
  →  2 post more than once
= 80–250 waitlist emails
```
**Four posts from the right four people is a success.** Plan for that, not for virality.
**The compounding part:** each creator who posts gives you a testimonial, a piece of content to repost, and social proof for the next pitch. Pitch #40 is far easier than pitch #1 because you can say "I've worked with [names]."

## 8. Organic engine — the free channels, ranked
### 1. Pinterest (highest ROI, most ignored)
Pinterest is a **search engine**, not a social feed. A pin from September still delivers traffic in December — the opposite of Instagram.
- 5 boards: *Puberty Talk for Parents* · *Raising Confident Black Girls* · *Tween Girl Mom Life* · *Printables & Journals* · *Mia's World*
- **3–5 pins/day, scheduled** (Workhorse drafts, you approve Monday, scheduler runs it)
- Keyword-stuff the *pin title + description + board name* with real search phrases: "how to talk to your daughter about puberty," "period kit for 10 year old," "puberty book for girls"
- Every pin → the free Question Kit landing page
- Vertical 1000×1500, text overlay readable at thumbnail size
- **Start Block 1.** Pinterest takes 4–8 weeks to warm up — which is exactly why starting it in November is useless.
### 2. Instagram + TikTok (trust-building)
5 posts/week across the 4 pillars. Your face 2×/week minimum. Use **IG Collab posts** with creators — it publishes to both audiences and is the single best free reach mechanic on the platform.
### 3. Facebook groups (slow, high-intent)
Join 6–8 Black-mom / girl-mom / homeschool groups **now**. Be a human for 3–4 weeks. Answer questions. Never drop a link cold — most will ban you. When someone asks "how do I talk to my daughter about her period," *that's* your moment, and it converts better than any ad.
### 4. Etsy + TPT search
These platforms hand you buyers. **List SKU 1 by early October** so ranking and reviews accrue before Black Friday. Keyword research is a Cowork job; listing copy is a Cowork draft; the actual listing form is Chrome-with-you-watching.
### 5. Your email list
The only audience you own. Everything above exists to feed it.

## 9. Rules — non-negotiable
1. 🔴 **You send every message.** No agent DMs, emails, or comments as you. Cowork drafts into `02-inbox/marketing/outreach/`; you copy, personalize, send.
2. **FTC disclosure** on every gifted collaboration — #gifted or #ad. Protects you both.
3. **No child photos, names, schools, or stories** from any creator's kids, ever. Not in a repost, not in a testimonial.
4. **No creator makes medical claims** on your behalf. Give them approved language. A well-meaning "this book explains exactly what's happening to her body" is a claim you haven't cleared yet.
5. **No paid Amazon reviews.** Gifted-for-honest-review with disclosure only.
6. **Written permission before you repost or quote** anyone. Save it in the vault.
7. **Never DM a minor.** Adults only, always, no exceptions.
8. **One follow-up, then stop.** Reputation compounds in a small niche.
